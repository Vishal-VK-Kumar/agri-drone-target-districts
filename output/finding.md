**In 2023-24, half of India's modelled crop-spraying demand sat in one tenth of
district-seasons. The top 50 are all Kharif, and most of them are soybean and cotton
districts in Maharashtra and western Madhya Pradesh.**

Spray demand here means sown acres of each in-scope crop multiplied by how many times
that crop is sprayed in a season, summed by district and season (acre-passes). Crop
areas are from UPAg's district-level APY dataset, 2023-24, the latest complete year.

| | Share of national demand | Share of district-seasons |
|---|---|---|
| Top 10 | 6.2% | 0.5% |
| Top 50 | 21.9% | 2.7% |
| Top decile (189) | 49.6% | 10.0% |

The top 50 split 19 Maharashtra, 15 Madhya Pradesh, 6 Rajasthan, 4 Punjab and 6
elsewhere. Soybean and cotton together are 72.4% of their demand (soybean 47.0%, cotton
25.4%). Yavatmal ranks first, then Beed, Nanded and Buldhana.

Rice is the largest source of demand nationally, at 37.4%, but only 11.0% of the top
50. Its demand is spread thin: about 115 district-seasons carry half of it, against
about 18 for soybean and 23 for cotton. The first rice-led district is Ludhiana, at
rank 28.

**How stable the ranking is.** Every crop's spray count has a low, base and high value,
each sourced. Of the top 50 at base, 40 are still in the top 50 at both low and high.
Of the top 10, 7 are. Yavatmal is first at all three.

**What it rests on.** Soybean carries almost half of the top 50 on a count of 3 sprays
per season, taken from the ICAR-NSRI schedule. Farmer surveys on insecticide and herbicide use
together imply 2.5 to 3.3 passes, depending on how many sprays are tank-mixed. Cotton
uses a survey mean of 2.40 and rice a state-level survey (CSISA 2018, 8,355 farmers). Three crops still have an open count:
maize, gram and moong, together 18.7% of national demand. Seven of the top 50 take more
than a quarter of their demand from them. The largest is Nagaur (rank 5, 72.7% moong),
which falls to rank 18 at the low count.

**What this does not answer.** The ranking measures where demand is thickest, not
whether one drone pays. A single drone serves a small fraction of any top-decile
district's demand, so the question that decides the business is the break-even model
in the Power BI layer, which works from the same data.
